What Kids Face Online
The Landscape

The internet wasn't designed for kids.

One idea to anchor the night: the internet wasn't designed with kids in mind. It was designed for adults (and it wasn't designed for privacy either), and kids are wedged into it. That creates three categories of risk: content they weren't meant to see, contact with people who aren't who they say they are, and platforms engineered to maximize time and spending. The good news — every device and platform has controls built in. They're just buried. Our job tonight is to find them.

Router Controls
Router Controls

The router is a good place to start.

Xfinity: use the Xfinity app. Create a profile per child, assign their devices, set a bedtime. Internet turns off automatically. Verizon: My Verizon app → Home Network → Parental Controls. Block categories, set times, pause devices. Both are free. Both take under ten minutes. Important caveat: this protects the home network only. Cellular data and away-from-home WiFi are not covered. So pair with device controls. Mention consoles too — Nintendo, PlayStation, Xbox all have family settings for play time and chat restrictions.

DNS Filtering
DNS Filtering · Free · ~5 min

Two numbers, and harmful sites stop loading.

DNS is the internet's phone book. When you type instagram-dot-com, your router asks a DNS server for the address. Change which phone book your router uses, and whole categories of harmful sites never load. OpenDNS FamilyShield: two numbers, memorize these — two-zero-eight dot sixty-seven dot two-twenty-two dot one-two-three and dot two-twenty dot one-two-three. CleanBrowsing is a stricter alternative. Log into your router at one-nine-two dot one-six-eight dot one dot one, paste the numbers in. Five minutes. If you're on Xfinity the gateway is locked, so use the Xfinity app filters from the previous slide instead. This is on the handout.

Choosing a First Device
Apple vs. Android

A first device is a chance to teach the ropes before they're on their own.

The first device matters a lot. It shapes habits. Apple is my strong recommendation — and I say that having worked across the industry. The reason is simple: content filtering works at the operating system level, so every browser is filtered, not just Safari. Family Sharing is mature. Ask to Buy works. The ecosystem is locked down. Android is cheaper and Family Link is a capable free tool, but web filtering only covers Chrome. A determined kid installs Firefox and the controls are gone. Not a dealbreaker — but worth knowing.

Apple Watch
Ages 8–12 · The sleeper pick

An Apple Watch covers calls and texts without handing over a phone.

The Apple Watch is the sleeper pick. Family Setup lets you give your child a watch without an iPhone. It pairs to YOUR phone. They can call and text approved contacts, you see location, Schooltime locks it during class, Emergency SOS always works. No browser. No App Store. No social. Around two-fifty for the watch, ten a month for cellular. For the eight-to-twelve range, when kids need to reach you but aren't ready for a phone — this is the answer.

Child Accounts
The one we almost made

The account matters more than the device.

The single biggest mistake parents make: letting kids use the parent's Apple ID, or creating an account with an adult birthday. That disables every parental control. You need a supervised child account with their real age. On Apple: Family Sharing, add member. On Google: families-dot-google-dot-com. A bonus tip: seed their algorithms. Before handing over the device, watch good content together. A fresh account gets the worst recommendations because the algorithm is guessing. A seeded history reinforces quality.

Locked-Down iPhone
Ages 10–14 · iPhone as a dumb phone

Already have an iPhone? Five minutes in Screen Time.

If your child already has an iPhone, or one's been handed down — you don't need a new device. Five minutes in Screen Time turns any iPhone into a phone that calls, texts, and takes photos. The key moves: create a child Apple ID, turn on Content and Privacy Restrictions, disable Safari, App Store, Mail. Communication Limits to contacts only. Set a Screen Time passcode they don't know — I cannot stress this enough. Start fully locked down. Earn trust, unlock one app at a time. Far easier than the reverse.

Screen Time
Apple Screen Time

Four Screen Time settings, and most of it's handled.

Screen Time is where you actually control what the device does day-to-day. Four things to know. Downtime — blocks apps on a schedule, set it for bedtime and school. Communication Limits — restrict calls and texts to approved contacts only, even during Downtime. Content & Privacy plus Web Filtering — block adult sites, restrict explicit music, add TikTok, Instagram, Snapchat to Never Allow. This filters at the OS level — all browsers. Always Allowed — the apps that work during Downtime. Phone, Maps, whatever educational app you approve. Again: passcode they don't know. Without it, they turn it all off.

Family Computers
When they need a real computer

If you can, stay in one ecosystem.

Phones are consumption. Computers are creation. Your kid will need a real computer for school, projects, learning to code. My advice: stay in the Apple ecosystem. Same Screen Time, same Family Sharing, same rules — controls sync automatically. Two quick wins. One: create a Standard account, not Admin. They can't install software or change settings without your password. Two: web filtering from the iPhone carries over. Same rules, no reconfiguration.

Closing
Your plan

You've got this.

Tonight's call to action is simple. Tonight: change your DNS. Five minutes, free, protects everyone. This week: lock down one device — Family Sharing plus Screen Time, or Family Link. Not all the devices. One. Always: talk to your kids. Tell them what you set up and why. Controls are scaffolding — the goal is independence. Technology isn't the enemy. Kids who learn to use computers well will have a superpower. Your job is to build the guardrails so they can explore safely and develop good judgment.
