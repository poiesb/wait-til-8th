The Landscape 0216
The Landscape

The internet wasn't
designed for kids.

It was designed for adults — and kids are wedged into it. Three categories of risk they navigate without guardrails:

Content

Algorithms push from cartoons to extreme material in minutes. Instagram is linked to body image issues in 1 in 3 teen girls — per Meta's own research.

Contact

Group chats with strangers by default. Predators pose as peers in games and social apps.

Manipulation

Loot boxes, limited-time skins, ads blended into content. Designed to maximize time, not safety.

Section 1

Home WiFi

The easiest win. Five minutes, free, and it protects every device on your network.

Home WiFi 0416
Router Controls

The router is a good
place to start.

Comcast / Xfinity

xFi app

  • Create a profile per child, assign their devices
  • Set a bedtime — internet turns off automatically
  • Pause one device or all with one tap
Verizon Fios / 5G

My Verizon app

  • Block content categories, set time restrictions
  • Pause individual devices
  • Works on Fios and 5G Home
The catch Home WiFi only. Not cellular data. Not away from home. Pair with device controls — that's the rest of tonight.

Section 2

First Devices

The device you choose shapes the habits your kid develops. Pick one with strong boundaries built in.

First Devices 0716
Apple vs. Android

A first device is a chance to teach
the ropes before they're on their own.

Recommended

Apple

  • OS-level content filtering — every browser, not just Safari
  • Family Sharing — manage everything from your phone
  • Ask to Buy — every app needs your approval
  • Locked-down ecosystem — harder for kids to find workarounds
Alternative

Android

  • Lower cost — solid options under $200
  • Family Link is powerful and free
  • App purchase approvals work well
  • Web filtering only covers Chrome — a real gap
First Devices 0816
Ages 8–12 · The sleeper pick

An Apple Watch covers calls and texts
without handing over a phone.

Cellular watch pairs to your iPhone and gets its own phone number. Your child can call and text — no browser, no App Store, no social media.

How it works

Child doesn't need their own iPhone — the watch pairs to yours but gets its own phone number. You manage every setting remotely.

Apple Watch SE: ~$249 + ~$10/mo cellular

What you control

  • Approved contacts only — calls & texts
  • Schooltime mode locks it during class
  • Location always visible to you
  • Emergency SOS always available
First Devices 0916
The one we almost made

The account matters more
than the device.

Critical Letting your child sign in with your account — or a new account with an adult birthday — disables every parental control. You need a supervised child account with their real age.
Apple

Family Sharing

  • Create via Family Sharing on your iPhone
  • Under-13s can't create their own — you must
  • Use their real birthday — controls are age-based
Google

Family Link

  • Create at families.google.com
  • Under-13s get a supervised account automatically
  • At 13, supervision lifts — set expectations early
First Devices 1016
Ages 10–14 · iPhone as a dumb phone

Already have an iPhone?
Five minutes in Screen Time.

With the child account in place, start fully locked down. Loosen one app at a time as trust is earned — far easier than the reverse.

What to keep on

  • Phone & Messages
  • FaceTime
  • Camera & Photos
  • Maps
  • Emergency SOS

What to turn off

  • Safari (no web at all)
  • App Store
  • Mail & Wallet
  • Social media & YouTube
  • Any installed game
Also worth setting Communication Limits lets you choose who your child can call or text — limit to contacts you approve, both during the day and during downtime.
First Devices 1116
Apple Screen Time

Four Screen Time settings,
and most of it's handled.

01

Downtime

Blocks all apps on a schedule. Set it for bedtime and school hours.

02

Communication Limits

Restrict calls and texts to approved contacts — even during Downtime.

03

Content & Privacy

Block adult sites, set age ratings. Works across all browsers.

04

Always Allowed

The apps that still work during Downtime. Phone, Maps, approved educational.

Non-negotiable Set a Screen Time passcode they don't know. Without it, they turn everything off.
First Devices 1216
When they need a real computer

If you can,
stay in one ecosystem.

Phones are consumption. Computers are creation — for school, projects, learning to code. Pick the path where controls already work.

Recommended

Mac

  • Same Screen Time, same Family Sharing, same rules
  • Web filtering from iPhone carries over automatically
  • Set up: System Settings → Screen Time → child's name
Two quick wins

On any computer

  • Standard account, not Admin — they can't install or change settings
  • Set a Screen Time passcode they don't know
  • Windows & Linux: parental controls are weaker — plan accordingly

Do these in order. You don't need to finish everything tonight — start with WiFi, then one device. Check off what's done. Come back for more next week.

① Home WiFi · ~10 min · Free

1.Open your ISP app: Xfinity or My Verizon. Enable parental controls.
2.Create a profile per child. Assign their devices.
3.Set a WiFi bedtime per device.
4.Change DNS to OpenDNS FamilyShield:
208.67.222.123  ·  208.67.220.123
5.Xfinity DNS is locked — use the app filters instead.
Xfinity guideSet up parental controls
Verizon Fios guideParental controls FAQs

② Create a Child Account · ~5 min

1.Apple: Settings → [Your Name] → Family Sharing → Add Member.
2.Google: families.google.com or Family Link app.
3.Always use real birthday — controls are age-based.
4.Never let them use your account, or an adult-age account.

③ First Device · Pick One

•Apple Watch Family Setup (ages 8–12) — calls, texts, location. No browser, no apps.
•iPad (Wi-Fi only) — safest first screen device.
•iPhone, locked down (ages 10–14) — Screen Time below.

④ Screen Time Setup · ~10 min

1.Enable Downtime — bedtime & school hours.
2.Turn on Communication Limits → contacts only.
3.Content & Privacy → Limit Adult Websites.
4.Add sites to Never Allow: tiktok.com, instagram.com, snapchat.com.
5.Set a Screen Time passcode they do not know.

⑤ Family Computer · ~5 min

1.On Mac: System Settings → Screen Time. Controls sync from iPhone.
2.Create a Standard user account — not Admin.
3.Set a Screen Time passcode they do not know.

⑥ Talk With Your Kids

•Tell them what you set up — and why.
•Agree on tech-free zones (meals, bedroom, car).
•Loosen one rule at a time as trust grows.
