What Kids Face Online

The internet is an incredible tool for learning and creativity — but it wasn't designed with kids in mind. Here's what they're navigating without guardrails.

Content

  • YouTube autoplay pushes kids from cartoons to extreme content in minutes
  • Instagram linked to body image issues in 1 in 3 teen girls (Meta's own research)
  • TikTok serves a new video every 15–30 seconds, each one optimized for your child's triggers
  • Adult content accessible on the open web with no age verification

Contact

  • Discord and Roblox put kids in group chats with strangers by default
  • Gaming platforms have unsupervised messaging
  • Predators pose as peers in games and social apps

Manipulation

  • Games like Fortnite use psychological tricks to drive spending — loot boxes, limited-time skins, peer pressure
  • TikTok and YouTube blend ads into content so kids can't tell the difference
  • Algorithms create addictive loops designed to maximize screen time, not safety
The good news: Every device and platform has parental tools built in. They're just buried in settings menus — tonight we dig them out.

Home WiFi Controls

The easiest win — protects every device on your network, takes 5 minutes, and it's free

📡

Your Router's Built-In Parental Controls

Comcast / Xfinity (xFi)

  • Open the Xfinity app → Connected Devices
  • Create a profile per child, assign their devices
  • Set a bedtime per device — internet turns off automatically
  • Pause one device or all with one tap
  • Setup guide: xfinity.com/support/articles/set-up-parental-controls-with-comcast-networking

Verizon (Fios / 5G Home)

  • Open My Verizon app → Home Network → Parental Controls
  • Block content categories, set time restrictions
  • Pause individual devices
  • Setup guide: verizon.com/support/residential/internet/essentials/fios-parental-controls-faqs
Covers every device on your home WiFi — including visitors' devices. No app install needed. But it doesn't protect on cellular data or away from home. Always pair with device-level controls too.
Gaming consoles too: Use Nintendo Switch Parental Controls, PlayStation Family Management, or Xbox Family Settings to set play time limits, restrict chat, and filter by age rating.
🔒

Free DNS Filtering: 5 Minutes, Big Impact

DNS is like the internet's phone book — it translates website names into addresses. By switching which "phone book" your router uses, harmful sites get blocked before they ever load. Free, no app needed, about 5 minutes.

OpenDNS FamilyShield (Free)

  • Set your router's DNS to: 208.67.222.123
  • Secondary: 208.67.220.123
  • Automatically blocks adult content network-wide
  • No account needed

CleanBrowsing (Free)

  • Family filter DNS: 185.228.168.168
  • More aggressive — also blocks mixed-content sites
  • Free tier covers all basic family needs

Verizon Fios

  • Open myfiosgateway.com in your browser
  • Password is printed on the side of your router
  • Broadband Connection → DNS Server → enter numbers above

Xfinity

  • DNS is locked on Xfinity gateways
  • Use the Xfinity app's content filters instead (previous slide)
  • Or change DNS on each device via Settings → Wi-Fi → DNS
Other routers: Try 192.168.1.1 or 10.0.0.1 in your browser → find DNS settings → replace with the numbers above. Call your ISP if stuck.

First Devices

iPhone · iPad · Mac · Apple Watch — the gold standard for built-in parental controls

🥇

First Devices

Start with a device that's safe to explore — one with strong boundaries built in, so kids can learn the ropes and develop good habits before they're on their own.

🍎 Apple

  • OS-level content filtering — all browsers, not just Safari
  • Family Sharing — manage everything from your phone
  • Ask to Buy — every app needs your approval
  • Locked-down ecosystem — harder for kids to find workarounds

🤖 Android

  • Lower cost — good options under $200
  • Family Link app is powerful and free
  • App purchase approvals work well
  • Web filtering limited to Chrome only

Top Recommendations

  • iPad (Wi-Fi only) — safest first device. No cellular, all content filtering built in.
  • Apple Watch (Family Setup) — call, text, and location only. No browser, no App Store, no social media.
⌚

Apple Watch Family Setup: A Safer First Device

The idea: An Apple Watch with cellular lets kids call and text parents — with no browser, no App Store, and no social media.

How It Works

  • Child doesn't need their own iPhone
  • Watch pairs to your iPhone instead
  • You manage all settings remotely
  • Apple Watch SE ~$249 + cellular ~$10/mo

What You Control

  • Approved contacts only — calls and texts
  • Schooltime mode locks it during class
  • Location always visible to you
  • No web browser, no App Store
  • Emergency SOS always available
Best for ages 8–12 who need to reach parents but aren't ready for a smartphone. Safety and connection — without the social media risk.
👶

Set Up a Child Account

Apple and Google both let you create a supervised child account — this is what unlocks all the parental controls. Without it, your child has an unrestricted adult account.

The #1 mistake: Letting your child sign in with your account or an account with an adult birthday. This disables all parental controls. Always create a separate child account with their real age.

🍎 Apple ID

  • Create via Family Sharing on your iPhone
  • Under-13s can't create their own — you must do it
  • Use their real birthday — controls are age-based
  • At 13, they can request to remove supervision — you'll get a notification and can approve or decline

🤖 Google

  • Create at families.google.com or via Family Link
  • Under-13s get a supervised account automatically
  • At 13, supervision lifts automatically — your child chooses to keep or remove it. Set expectations early.
  • Works on Android, Chromebook, and web
👨‍👩‍👧

Setting Up a Family

This is step one. Family Sharing is free and connects your family's Apple IDs. Every parental control in the next slides — Screen Time, content filtering, Ask to Buy — requires this to be set up first.
  1. Settings → [Your Name] → Family Sharing → Add Member
  2. Create your child's Apple ID with their real birthday
  3. You become Family Organizer — you approve every app download
  4. Manage all controls remotely from your own phone

Ask to Buy

Every app download — even free ones — needs your approval first.

Find My

See your child's device location at any time. Transparency builds trust.

apple.com/family-sharing

Apple's official guide with setup walkthrough, FAQs, and supported features.

⏱️

Apple Screen Time & Content Filtering

Settings → Screen Time on your child's device — or manage remotely from your Settings → Screen Time → [child's name]

1 Downtime

Blocks all apps on a schedule. Set it for bedtime and school hours — only apps you approve stay available.

2 Communication Limits

Restrict calls and texts to approved contacts only — even during Downtime. Keeps strangers out.

3 Content & Privacy + Web Filtering

Block adult websites, restrict explicit music, set age ratings. Filtering works at the OS level — all browsers, not just Safari. Add tiktok.com, instagram.com, snapchat.com to the "Never Allow" list.

4 Always Allowed

Apps that work during Downtime — Phone, Maps, and educational apps you specifically approve.

Critical step: Set a Screen Time passcode they don't know — without it, they can turn everything off.
💻

Getting a Real Computer

Computers are different. An iPad is a consumption device — a Mac is a creation device. Your child will need it for school, projects, and learning to code.
Why Mac: Staying in the Apple ecosystem keeps parental controls simple — same Screen Time, same Family Sharing, same rules. Macs can do everything a Windows PC can (often more), and they're the primary computer for most AI and software developers. Windows and Linux lack reliable built-in parental controls.

Setup (2 minutes)

  1. Open System Settings → Screen Time
  2. Select your child's name from Family Sharing
  3. Same controls as iPhone sync automatically
  4. Set a Screen Time passcode they don't know

Two Quick Wins

Use a Standard Account — not Admin. Your child can't install software or change settings without your password.

Web filtering carries over — the same content rules from iPhone apply automatically.
